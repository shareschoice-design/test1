How the picker works (and what the maths says)

You bet 4 straight-up numbers from anywhere, one chip each. A hit pays 35:1 on that number, so the net win is 8× the total stake. The chance that one of 4 numbers lands is always 4/37 = 10.81%.

Each number gets four characteristics:

1 · γ (gap speed). v = 1 − P(gap), where P(gap) = (36/37)gap; γ = 1/√(1−v²). The longer the number has not landed, the faster γ grows.
2 · m (coldness). m = 1 + 0.25·max(0, −z). Numbers hitting less than expected get heavier.
3 · g (wheel gravity). Every landing adds “gravity” to its wheel neighbours, fading by 20% per spin.
4 · b (group pressure). How far behind expectation the number's colour, dozen, column and odd/even are.

Score E = γ · m · (1+g) · (1+b) (switched-off factors count as 1). The 4 numbers with the highest E are the picks.

Timing: the secretary rule. For the first 37% of the wait limit it only watches and remembers the best total E of the top 4. After that it bets the first time the top 4 beat that best (and, with filter 1 on, a neighbour just landed). When the wait limit is reached it bets the current top 4 anyway. The window is 1–20 spins, longer when the picks' gaps are longer, and the recovery progression makes every hit end in profit.

Important: the wheel has no memory. A number that has missed 150 spins still has exactly 1/37 on the next one. That is why “chance” on the bet card comes from 10.81% per spin, not from the scores. Every bet has an expected value of −2.70%. The Strategy lab compares the picker with random numbers on the same spins so you can check this yourself. Only play with money you can afford to lose.
